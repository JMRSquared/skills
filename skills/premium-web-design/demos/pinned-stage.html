One knife, eleven weeks.

Rob Sandell and Nita Vaughan draw every blade from bar stock in a shop off Malin Bridge. Nothing is cut from plate, and nothing leaves without both their marks on the tang.

01

The billet

Bar stock arrives as 1.2519 — a tungsten tool steel that takes a fine edge and forgives a slow quench. Rob draws it down over four heats at 1,050 °C, then buries it in vermiculite overnight so the grain settles before anything is cut.

Four heats · 1,050 °C · normalised 14 hours

02

The grind

The hollow is cut on a 610 mm wheel and then taken back by hand, 40 grit through to 600. The edge is left 0.3 mm at the shoulder before heat treat — thin enough to cut cleanly, thick enough to survive the oil.

0.3 mm at the shoulder · 62 HRC after temper

03

The handle

Nita shapes stabilised walnut from a tree felled on Rivelin Valley Road, pinned in brass and bedded on G-flex. The last forty minutes are 1500 grit and a thumb, worked against a tracing of the hand that ordered it.

Walnut, Rivelin Valley · brass pin · hand-fitted
