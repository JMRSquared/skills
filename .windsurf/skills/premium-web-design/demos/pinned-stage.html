Rob forges every blade from a bar of tool steel

The bar is 1.2519, a tungsten tool steel that takes a fine edge and forgives a slow quench. Rob draws it down over four heats at 1,050 °C. Then he buries it in vermiculite overnight so the grain settles before anything is cut.

Four heats at 1,050 °C, then 14 hours to normalise

We grind each edge by hand, from 40 grit to 600

Rob cuts the hollow on a 610 mm wheel and takes it back by hand. He leaves the edge 0.3 mm thick at the shoulder before heat treatment. That is thin enough to cut cleanly and thick enough to survive the oil quench.

0.3 mm at the shoulder, 62 HRC after tempering

Nita shapes a walnut handle to fit your hand

She uses stabilised walnut from a tree felled on Rivelin Valley Road, pinned in brass and bedded on epoxy. The last forty minutes are 1500 grit and her thumb, worked against a tracing of the hand that ordered the knife.

Rivelin Valley walnut, brass pin, fitted by hand
