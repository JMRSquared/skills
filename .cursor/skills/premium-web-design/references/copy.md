# Words

Most of a good page is not text. The real sites we measured run 17-43 words per screen with 80-281 words in total: Hagi's barbershop has 80, Fizzi 174, Tripletta 178, Pizzato 281. The more an agent writes, the more the page reads as generated, so the job is to write less.

## Budgets

The auditor fails a page over 300 words, not counting nav, footer or anything inside `data-reference`.

| Where | Limit |
|---|---|
| Hero | A headline of 8 words or fewer, at most one sentence under it, one button. Nothing else |
| Section heading | 6 words or fewer, plain. Many sections need no heading at all |
| Section body | 25 words or fewer, often none. A photo with a caption is enough |
| Sections | 3-5 after the hero |

Put `data-reference` on content people come to look up: a price list, a menu, opening hours, a spec table. It does not count against the budget. Only real data from the brief qualifies. Do not use it to smuggle in prose.

## What never goes on the page

Delete on sight. These add words without adding information:

- Eyebrows and labels above headings
- A note beside or under a button ("No card needed", "We reply within the hour")
- A subheading that restates the heading
- Stickers, badges, tickets and floating data cards
- Marquees and ticker bands
- Section numbers (01, 02, 03)
- "How it works" steps, unless the process is what confuses people
- An FAQ, unless the brief supplies the questions
- Stats, testimonials or client lists, unless the brief supplies them
- Scroll hints, closing taglines and a sign-off line in the footer

## Facts

Every fact comes from the brief or the client's existing material: names, prices, hours, numbers, quotes, claims. **Never invent one.** That includes the little realistic details that make generated pages feel fake: ticket numbers, timestamps, measurements, street corners, staff first names, sample chats, a fictional customer's quote.

Missing a fact? Leave it out. If the page cannot work without it, ask, or use one visibly marked placeholder such as `[phone number]` and list it in your reply.

## How to write the little that remains

Use the client's own words first, taken from the brief or their current site, even if they are plain. If you must write, say the plain thing a person behind the counter would say.

| Generated | Written |
|---|---|
| Same-day puncture fixes. Even to "it makes a noise". | Bike repairs in Woodstock. |
| Your bike. Back on the road by lunch. | Drop it off by 10. Collect it the same day. |
| Not just a haircut. A ritual. | Haircuts and shaves. |
| Crafted with care, built to last. | We make oak tables to order. |
| Built for the way you work. | Accounting software for small builders. |

**The tells to search your draft for:**

- Wit or a wink the brief did not ask for. Humour is the fastest way to sound generated. Play it straight unless the client is funny and says so.
- Short fragments with full stops ("No fuss. No waiting. Just coffee.").
- Contrasts ("not X, but Y", "less X, more Y").
- Three of anything in a row.
- A closing line that sums up or lands a point.
- Puffery: elevate, seamless, unlock, crafted, curated, bespoke, journey, effortless, thoughtfully, timeless, modern, reimagined.
- Questions as headings ("Ready to ride?").
- Addressing the reader's feelings ("You deserve", "Imagine").
- Em dashes.

A short plain label is a good heading: "Prices", "Services", "Find us". A clever fragment is not: "Already booked." "Five formats." Test: read the headings alone, in order. They should describe the business like a table of contents, not like an ad.

## When the user asks you to remove or change text

This is the most common follow-up, so follow it exactly.

- **"Remove", "delete", "too much text", "less text" means delete.** Delete the element, then fix the layout so nothing looks missing. Do not write a shorter replacement. Do not move the idea elsewhere on the page.
- **"This sounds like AI" also means delete first.** Remove the line and look at the page. Only if the page truly needs words there, use the client's own wording or the plainest possible sentence, and say that you did.
- Touch only what they pointed at, then report what you deleted in one line. Do not rewrite neighbouring copy.
- Never answer a complaint about text with new text you did not need.
